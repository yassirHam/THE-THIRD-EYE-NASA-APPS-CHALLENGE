#	Principle	What it means
1	Delete filler badges	Remove unnecessary tags such as “AI-Powered,” “Next-Gen,” or “Premium” that add no useful information.
2	Drop redundant labels	Avoid repeating information already communicated by headings, icons, or surrounding elements.
3	Unitalicize random words	Stop applying italics to arbitrary words purely for decoration. Use typography intentionally.
4	Mono only where needed	Reserve monospace fonts for code, technical values, identifiers, and appropriate data displays.
5	Use a design system	Establish consistent typography, colors, spacing, border radii, and reusable components.
6	Show the actual product	Replace vague illustrations and generic graphics with real product screenshots or functional previews.
7	Enlarge the main feature	Make the most important product feature visually dominant rather than giving every element equal weight.
8	Group with spacing	Use proximity and whitespace to establish relationships between elements instead of excessive borders or containers.
9	Make headlines specific	Replace generic marketing language with concrete descriptions of what the product does or delivers.
10	Emphasize the CTA	Make the primary call-to-action visually prominent, clearly worded, and easy to identify.
11	Add an accent colour	Introduce a deliberate accent color to guide attention and establish visual identity.
12	Keep icons consistent	Use one coherent icon family with consistent stroke widths, sizes, and visual styles.
13	Remove embellishments	Eliminate unnecessary gradients, glowing effects, decorative blobs, shadows, and visual ornaments.
14	Inner radius = outer − padding	For nested rounded containers, use geometrically consistent corner radii. For uniform padding, a useful approximation is inner radius = outer radius − padding.
15	Improve text contrast	Ensure text is readable against its background, especially secondary labels and body text.
16	Wrap headlines properly	Control line breaks to produce balanced, readable headlines rather than awkward word wrapping.
17	Align by eye, not pixels	Use optical alignment when mathematically perfect positioning still looks visually unbalanced.
18	Use tabular nums	Enable tabular numerals for dashboards, counters, tables, and financial values so numbers maintain consistent widths.
19	Add loading states	Show meaningful progress indicators, skeleton screens, or loading feedback during asynchronous operations.
20	Animate useful feedback	Use subtle motion to communicate changes, confirmations, transitions, or interaction states rather than decorative animation.
